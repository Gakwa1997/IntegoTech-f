# IntegoTech — Brand Positioning & Homepage Architecture

**Prepared:** September 2026
**Purpose:** Reposition IntegoTech from a local IT support provider into an authority-led
Digital Transformation consultancy — credible to Kigali schools and SMEs today, and
credible to international remote clients tomorrow.

---

## 0. The Strategic Problem

The previous homepage led with *"Your school's IT department — without the cost of hiring one."*

That line converts well with a bursar. But it does three things that cap the business:

1. **It anchors on cost avoidance**, not capability. You become the cheap alternative to
   hiring — a price position, not an authority position.
2. **It is single-vertical.** A hotel, a clinic or a UK startup reading that line assumes
   the service is not for them.
3. **It is geographically bounded.** "Kigali" in the hero tells an international buyer
   the ceiling of your ambition before they read a word about your capability.

The fix is not to abandon schools. Schools remain the beachhead. The fix is to **raise the
category** you compete in — from *IT support* to *digital transformation* — and let the
school offer sit inside that as a named programme.

**The reframe in one line:**

> We are not the people you call when the printer breaks.
> We are the people who take an organisation from paper to platform.

## 1. Brand Positioning & Taglines — 3 Options

### Option A — "Systems That Scale" ⭐ RECOMMENDED

| | |
|---|---|
| **Tagline** | Smart Systems. Reliable Cloud. Real Global Reach. |
| **Hero headline** | We build the systems organisations run on. |
| **Positioning** | Engineering-led. You are the builder, not the maintainer. |

**Why this wins.** It is the only option that carries all three revenue lines
(websites, custom systems, cloud) in a single sentence without listing them. "Run on"
is load-bearing language — it says mission-critical, which is what justifies a
consultancy rate. It also preserves the motto you already committed to and have in
your visual identity, so nothing is wasted.

**Risk to manage:** slightly abstract on its own. It needs the sub-headline to land
the concrete offer, which is why the sub-headline below leads with the tangible.

---

### Option B — "From Paper to Platform"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Tagline** | From paper to platform. |
| **Hero headline** | Your operations, rebuilt for the digital era. |
| **Positioning** | Transformation-led. Sells the *journey*, not the technology. |

**Why it works.** This is the single most persuasive line for your *current* market.
A head teacher running admissions in ledgers understands it instantly and emotionally.
It also naturally introduces your 0 → 100 programme.

**Why it is not the recommendation.** "Paper to platform" implies the client is behind.
That is true of a Rwandan primary school; it is insulting to a London fintech that
might hire you for Entra ID work. It's a brilliant *section* headline — I've used it in
the SME spotlight — but as the master brand line it puts a ceiling on who can hire you.

---

### Option C — "The Engineering Standard"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Tagline** | Enterprise engineering, without the enterprise overhead. |
| **Hero headline** | Enterprise-grade technology, built for organisations of every size. |
| **Positioning** | Credibility-led. Trades explicitly on the Microsoft/Azure pedigree. |

**Why it works.** It is the most immediately *credible* option and the most defensible
against the "are you just a freelancer?" objection. It says: the standards are enterprise,
the access is not.

**Why it is not the recommendation.** "Without the enterprise overhead" is still a
cost-relative claim — the same trap as the old headline, dressed better. And it under-sells
the websites and custom systems work, which is where your local revenue actually comes from.

---

### Recommendation

**Ship Option A.** Keep Option B's language alive inside the SME/Schools section, where
its emotional pull does real work. Keep Option C's language alive in the Trust and
Who We Are sections, where credibility claims belong.

You get all three — but each in the register where it converts, rather than one
compromised blend in the hero.

## 2. Hero Section — Copy Strategy

**Eyebrow (context badge)**
> Digital Transformation · Custom Systems · Cloud Infrastructure

**Main headline**
> We build the systems organisations run on.

**Sub-headline**
> IntegoTech designs the websites, custom management platforms and cloud
> infrastructure that take institutions from manual and invisible to
> connected, secure and discoverable. Schools, growing businesses and
> enterprises — delivered on site or entirely remotely.

*Construction note:* the sub-headline does four jobs in one paragraph — names all three
pillars, names all three audiences, states the transformation outcome, and quietly
declares remote delivery capability. "Delivered on site or entirely remotely" is the
single most important clause on the page for international conversion; it removes the
geography objection before it is raised.

**Primary CTA:** `Book a Strategy Consultation →`
Not "Contact us" (passive), not "Get a quote" (commodity framing). "Strategy consultation"
sets the relationship as advisory from the first click and is the language a consultancy uses.

**Secondary CTA:** `View Digital Solutions`
Points to the platforms/systems pillar. This is deliberately the *builder* proof,
not the support proof — it is what a sceptical visitor wants to verify.

**Trust strip beneath the CTAs**
> ✓ Microsoft-certified engineering  ✓ Remote-first delivery  ✓ Security-first architecture

## 3. The Three Strategic Pillars

Everything you sell now resolves into three categories. This is the most important
structural change on the page — six flat service cards read as a task list;
three named pillars read as a practice.

### Pillar 01 — Digital Visibility & Platforms
*Be found. Be credible. Be online properly.*

Custom website design and build · Web applications and portals · Domain registration and
management · Hosting, SSL and DNS architecture · Google Business Profile and search visibility ·
Brand and digital identity · Multilingual and international-facing presentation

**The pitch:** Your website is the first thing a parent, client or partner judges you by.
We build ones that hold up — fast, secure, professionally written, and owned outright by you.

### Pillar 02 — Custom Operations & Management Systems
*Replace the paperwork with software built for how you actually work.*

School management systems — admissions, registration, marks, report cards, fees, parent portals ·
Booking and reservation platforms · Ticketing and helpdesk systems · Inventory and unit tracking ·
Lightweight ERP and workflow automation · Reporting dashboards · Role-based access and audit trails

**The pitch:** Off-the-shelf software forces you to change how you work. We build to your
process instead — then automate the parts that were eating your staff's week.

### Pillar 03 — Cloud Infrastructure & Strategic IT
*The foundation everything else depends on.*

Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace migration and administration · Microsoft Entra ID and
hybrid identity · Directory synchronisation and conditional access · Modern workplace security,
MFA and access governance · Network, server and backup architecture · Managed support retainers ·
Technology roadmapping and advisory

**The pitch:** This is the work our engineers do every day for organisations across the
world through Microsoft's support channels. The same standard, applied directly to you.

## 4. Schools & SME Modernization Spotlight

This section is the emotional core of the page and where local conversion happens.
Structure it as **Problem → Cost → Solution → Proof**, not as a feature list.

### The problem, named plainly

Three named symptoms, each phrased as the client would say it — not as a vendor would:

| Symptom | The line to use |
|---|---|
| **Invisible online** | "Parents search for your school and find nothing — or worse, find a page that hasn't been updated since 2019. Meanwhile the school down the road looks like an international institution." |
| **Running on paper** | "Admissions in ledgers. Marks in exercise books. Fees in a cash book. One flood, one fire, one lost file and years of records are gone — and nobody can produce a report when the board asks for one." |
| **Disconnected work** | "The bursar's spreadsheet doesn't match the registrar's list, which doesn't match what the head teacher was told. Everyone re-enters the same information into different files." |

*Why this phrasing:* it describes their Tuesday, not our product. A reader who recognises
their own week in your copy has already decided you understand the problem.

### The IntegoTech solution — the 0 → 100 Programme

Reframe your existing two-track model as the delivery vehicle:

- **Track A — Starting at Zero.** No website, no official email, no system, no backups.
  A fixed 90-day build that puts the whole foundation in place.
- **Track B — Already Running.** Systems exist but are underused, overpaid for, or exposed.
  We audit, harden, integrate and advance them.

Then the four deliverables, stated as outcomes:

1. **A website that earns trust** — professional, fast, found on Google, owned by you.
2. **A management system built to your process** — admissions to report cards, one connected flow.
3. **Official email and cloud identity** — every staff member on the school's own domain.
4. **Backups, security and documentation** — so nothing depends on one person's memory.

### The proof close

> Every engagement opens with a free audit. We visit, review every system, and give you a
> written report of what exists, what's at risk and what to fix first — and you keep that
> report whether or not you engage us.

*Why keep this:* it is your single strongest conversion mechanic. It converts a sales
conversation into a consulting deliverable before money changes hands, which is exactly
how high-end consultancies open. Do not dilute it.

## 5. Global Readiness — 4 Trust Signals for International Clients

An overseas buyer hiring a consultancy they will never meet in person is managing exactly
four anxieties. Each needs a visible answer on the homepage.

### 1. Verifiable engineering credentials
**The anxiety:** *"Are these people actually qualified, or is this a freelancer with a template site?"*

**What to show:** Microsoft-certified engineering, active enterprise identity and cloud
work delivered through Microsoft's own support channels, named lead consultant with a
real profile and a requestable CV. Certification badges near the fold, not buried in a footer.

**Why it's first:** every other reassurance is worthless if capability is in doubt.

---

### 2. Documented delivery methodology (SOPs)
**The anxiety:** *"Will this be improvised? Will I be chasing them for updates?"*

**What to show:** a named, repeatable engagement process — **Audit → Design → Build →
Deploy → Support** — with defined deliverables at each stage, written documentation
handed over at close, and change control on scope. State it as *"a documented delivery
methodology, not an improvised one."*

**Why it matters:** process visibility is what separates a consultancy from a contractor
in a buyer's mind. It is also the cheapest trust signal to add — you already work this way.

---

### 3. Security-first architecture & data ownership
**The anxiety:** *"Am I handing my organisation's data and admin access to someone I can't audit?"*

**What to show:** MFA and least-privilege access as standard on every build; role-based
access and audit trails in every system you deliver; encrypted backups with tested restores;
and — critically — an explicit **client data ownership** statement: domains registered in the
client's name, admin credentials held by the client, full documentation handed over,
no lock-in.

**Why it matters:** this is the one that closes regulated and education clients anywhere
in the world, and it costs you nothing because it is already true.

---

### 4. Remote-first delivery with stated availability
**The anxiety:** *"Different country, different time zone — how will this actually work day to day?"*

**What to show:** state the working window in **UTC** (not just local time), name the
collaboration stack you work in (Teams, email, shared documentation, ticketing), commit to a
response-time standard, and say plainly that the majority of the work — cloud administration,
system builds, security reviews, website delivery — requires no physical presence at all.

**Why it matters:** "we work remotely" is a claim. "Mon–Sat, 06:00–16:00 UTC, four-hour
response, delivered through Teams with written weekly reporting" is an operating model.
Buyers pay for the second one.

---

### Supporting placement notes

- **Geography moves down, it does not disappear.** Keep "Built in Kigali · Serving clients
  worldwide" as a *trust badge and footer line*. Kigali is an authenticity asset once
  capability has been established — it is a liability only when it arrives first.
- **Currency and pricing stay off the page.** Quote after the audit. This is correct for
  consultancy positioning and it also solves the international problem, since a
  price legible to a Kigali school reads as implausibly low to a European buyer.
- **The client logo strip stays greyscale until hover.** It reads as restraint rather
  than as a scramble for credibility. Replace placeholders only as each client consents.

## 6. Recommended Homepage Section Order

| # | Section | Job it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hero** | Category claim + both audiences + both CTAs |
| 2 | **Motto bar** | Brand line as a visual breath between sections |
| 3 | **Client strip** | Immediate social proof |
| 4 | **Three Pillars** | Reframes you from support to practice |
| 5 | **Schools & SME Spotlight** | Problem → cost → solution; local conversion |
| 6 | **0 → 100 Programme** | The delivery vehicle, made concrete |
| 7 | **Global Readiness** | The four international trust signals |
| 8 | **Engagement Method** | Documented methodology, stage by stage |
| 9 | **Support Partnerships** | The retainers, reframed as ongoing partnership |
| 10 | **Who We Are** | Named authority + credentials + skills |
| 11 | **FAQ** | Kills the six real objections |
| 12 | **Contact** | Strategy consultation booking |

**The single most important ordering decision:** pillars come *before* the school
spotlight. A visitor must understand you as a technology practice first, and only then
see schools as a served market. Reverse the order and you are a school IT company again,
regardless of what the copy says.
